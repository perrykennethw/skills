# Personality to visual system

Personality is a coordinated set of signals. Radius alone cannot make a site trustworthy, playful, premium, or serious.

## Gather evidence

Derive the direction from:

- the site's subject and primary job;
- the people using it and their emotional state;
- the cost or consequence of mistakes;
- frequency of use and expected information density;
- the brand traits that must remain recognizable;
- undesirable impressions such as childish, bureaucratic, frivolous, cold, or generic.

Distinguish trust from severity. A regulated or high-stakes product may need precision, but excessive austerity can make it feel hostile. Likewise, a friendly product still needs crisp interaction states and readable data.

## Write a personality profile

Record the target as a small set of tensions, using words or approximate positions rather than false precision:

- formal ↔ casual
- serious ↔ playful
- crisp ↔ soft
- restrained ↔ expressive
- dense ↔ spacious
- familiar ↔ distinctive

Then state:

- **Desired impression:** what a user should feel within the first few seconds.
- **Avoid:** two or three plausible but wrong impressions.
- **Reason:** how the direction supports the audience and primary task.

## Translate the profile

Choose each signal as part of the same system:

| Signal | More formal, serious, or crisp | More casual, playful, or soft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Radius | Square to modest corners; pills reserved for true capsules | Larger corners and more rounded controls |
| Typography | Restrained forms, controlled scale, stronger structure | Friendlier forms, more size contrast, looser expression |
| Color | Lower saturation, disciplined accents, stable neutrals | Broader or brighter accents, warmer or more varied surfaces |
| Spacing | Compact to moderate rhythm, clear alignment | More breathing room and looser compositions |
| Borders and shadows | Precise boundaries, limited elevation | Softer boundaries, broader elevation, layered surfaces |
| Imagery and icons | Functional, specific, consistent geometry | Expressive illustration, softer geometry, more character |
| Copy | Direct, reassuring, precise | Conversational, energetic, encouraging |

These are tendencies, not presets. Luxury editorial work can be spacious but square; a serious health product can be softly rounded to feel humane. The combination must be internally coherent and appropriate to the user.

## Useful starting ranges

Use these only to open the design conversation, then test them in the actual interface:

- Institutional or highly formal: roughly 0–4px component radii, restrained shadows.
- Professional and approachable: roughly 4–8px radii, subtle elevation.
- Friendly consumer product: roughly 8–16px radii, softer surface transitions.
- Deliberately playful: roughly 12–24px radii or selective capsules, expressive supporting signals.

Do not use one global radius for everything. Controls, cards, overlays, tags, and media frames may use related but distinct levels. Preserve recognizable control shapes and adequate hit areas.

## Example: insurance portal

A claims portal might target **formal, serious, crisp, restrained, moderately dense, and familiar**, while explicitly avoiding both *playful* and *bureaucratic*. A coherent result could use modest 2–4px radii, a highly readable humanist sans, calm low-saturation blues or greens, precise alignment, restrained elevation, plain reassuring copy, and strong feedback states.

Zero radius is also defensible when it matches the brand, but the industry alone does not require it.
